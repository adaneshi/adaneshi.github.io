The map

The outer box is the economy: investment, output, wages, and the stock of machines. The three circles are who produces. The middle is shared infrastructure. The papers start on the roads into that middle.

Production

An economy is people making goods and services and then using them. I keep the ordinary numbers in view: what gets invested, what gets produced, how much of income goes to labor, and how much capital sits behind each worker. AI enters that ledger as new plants, new machines, and a faster pass through jobs that used to take a room of people. It also enters as goods that were not on offer before.

Firms do most of the making. Government buys, regulates, and runs services of its own. Universities, the press, unions, and voters live with the result and sometimes build tools of their own. Where all three overlap, you get shared plumbing: a way to prove who someone is, a way to pay, a grid, a public computer researchers can use. That overlap is where I want the work to go. The papers I can finish now each start in one circle and point toward it.

Security sits where private production and public authority already share the machines. Most of what gets called critical infrastructure is privately owned. Chip plants, the power behind data centers, and the defense industrial base are production with a security reason attached: procurement, export rules, a decision about what may leave the country. A rival state, or a supply chain that can be cut, sits outside the three circles. That outside pressure is why the overlap is urgent. What I want on the ledger there is output, investment, and wages.

Three pieces of work

The thesis is the public circle. Federal agencies now publish lists of the AI they say they are using. I ask when it is faster to compute next to the work than to ship the work to a distant machine, and whether the writeups that sound like field work are the ones that actually get used. The lists are long on security agencies when the case touches rights or safety. They are quiet on whether those same cases need to run nearby. The plain account is here, and the draft is on SSRN.

A second paper, still being written, stays with firms. It asks whether signals of AI and automation move U.S. manufacturing, which is the private circle on the map.

A third, also still being written, stays with public life. It asks whether comments on federal rules changed once generative tools were easy to use, and whether that change can be forecast ahead of time.

I am lead researcher and co-founder of the Future Economies Kuwait Research Lab at Columbia. The lab's current work includes quantum infrastructure with the Kuwait Institute for Scientific Research, and conversations with the Eurasia Group's Institute for Global Affairs and with IBM Kuwait.

Path

I finished two Columbia degrees in 2025: an M.P.A. in International Security Policy and an M.A. in Quantitative Methods in the Social Sciences, in international finance and economic policy. Anya Schiffrin Stiglitz directed the technology, media, and communication specialization. Before that I studied economics and business at California State University, Chico, and received the 2016 Economics Outstanding Student Award.

From 2023 to 2025 I worked at the United Nations, across the Development Coordination Office, the Capital Development Fund, and the Permanent Mission of Afghanistan. I managed a Columbia SIPA capstone for UN DESA on development cooperation, and a quantitative-methods capstone for Consumer Reports.

Before graduate school I founded Saader and worked in enterprise sales, at Taygo, Rubrik, and Landed, and on two political campaigns. At Columbia I taught for Sigridur Benediktsdottir on Keynes and on macroprudential policy. At Chico I taught for Michael Perelman and worked as a research assistant for Ryan Miller.

In 2025 I spoke at the United Nations Open-Source Week on data protection in shared digital systems, on AI and work, and on how those systems get governed. I edited the student draft of "People-centered Intelligence for Human Development" for a UNDP workshop, and led sessions at the Columbia and UN roundtables in 2024 and 2025.
