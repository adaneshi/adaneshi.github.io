Research

My research concerns the adoption of artificial intelligence and automation and its place in national production, which I measure through the investment, output, and wages recorded in the national accounts. I work with public administrative records, among them the federal inventories of AI use cases, the Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey, trade statistics on imports of industrial robots and semiconductor equipment, and the dockets of federal rulemaking. In each paper I use only data that were published by the date of the forecast or test, and I compare the models with simple benchmarks, such as the forecast that production stays at its last value.

The papers cover three sectors. My thesis studies AI in the federal government, the first short paper studies U.S. manufacturing firms, and the second studies the comments that the public files on federal rules. I am also interested in the economics of security, since about 85 percent of U.S. critical infrastructure is privately owned, according to the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. Decisions about chip fabrication, the power supply of data centers, and the defense industrial base are therefore production decisions and decisions of public policy at the same time.

Working Papers

Conditional Value of Edge AI for U.S. Federal Agencies. Columbia thesis, advised by C. Cull, SSRN Working Paper 7141719.
Federal agencies publish annual inventories of the AI systems they use, and each system can run on a small computer at the site of use or on a remote cloud service. In a queueing model at published hardware values, the nearby computer is faster on average only while it is lightly loaded. Between two and four percent of filings each year describe the conditions associated with local processing, and of the 19 such use cases not in use in 2024 that I can follow into 2025, one was in use a year later. Summary.

Do Artificial Intelligence and Automation Signals Forecast U.S. Manufacturing? In preparation.
I ask whether survey rates of AI use and imports of robots and semiconductor equipment improve forecasts of industry production that could have been made at the time. The forecast that production stays at its last value is the most accurate at most horizons, and the technology records improve neither forecasts of production nor forecasts of whether it will fall.

Did Generative AI Move Civic Discourse, and Can That Movement Be Forecast? In preparation.
I ask whether weekly series of public comments at three federal agencies changed at the release of ChatGPT in November 2022. The share of Environmental Protection Agency comments labelled as mass campaigns rose at the release, and a simulation model of the campaign process attributes the change to the intensity of campaigns.

Background

I received two degrees from Columbia University in 2025, an M.P.A. in International Security Policy and an M.A. in Quantitative Methods in the Social Sciences, with a focus on international finance and economic policy. My specialization in technology, media, and communications was directed by Anya Schiffrin Stiglitz. Before that I studied economics and business at California State University, Chico, where I received the 2016 Economics Outstanding Student Award.

From 2023 to 2025 I worked at the United Nations, in the Development Coordination Office, the Capital Development Fund, and the Permanent Mission of Afghanistan. I managed a Columbia SIPA capstone for UN DESA on development cooperation and a quantitative-methods capstone for Consumer Reports. At Columbia I was a teaching assistant for Sigridur Benediktsdottir in courses on Keynes and on macroprudential policy, and at Chico I was a teaching assistant for Michael Perelman and a research assistant for Ryan Miller.

Before graduate school I founded Saader, worked in enterprise sales at Taygo, Rubrik, and Landed, and worked on two political campaigns. In 2025 I spoke at the United Nations Open Source Week on data protection in shared digital systems, on AI and work, and on the governance of these systems. I edited the student draft of "People-centered Intelligence for Human Development" for a UNDP workshop and led sessions at Columbia and United Nations roundtables in 2024 and 2025.
