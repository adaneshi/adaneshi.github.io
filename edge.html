Public AI, nearby or far away

Ashkaan Daneshi

Columbia University

Agencies now publish lists of the AI they say they are using. I ask when the work should be computed next to where it happens, and whether the cases that read like field work are the ones that get used.

The public circle

Where this sits

On the map, this paper is the public circle. Firms make most of the goods. Government buys, regulates, and runs services. This paper uses the public lists. Payments, identity, and the grid sit in the middle of the map, further along than these lists reach.

Security shows up here as a kind of production the government has to steer: policing, borders, benefits, and the agencies that file those cases. The question is whether that steering lines up with a practical choice about where the computer sits.

A short wire or a fast machine

Picture two ways to answer a request. One is a smaller machine next to the camera, the clerk, or the vehicle. The wire is short, so the answer starts coming back at once, but the machine itself is slow, and a queue forms quickly once it is busy. The other is a large machine in a distant hall. It works through requests faster, but every request spends time on the wire.

While the nearby machine is idle, the short wire wins. Once it is busy most of the time, requests wait, and the distant machine is quicker even after the trip. On the benchmark hardware in the paper, the two are even when the nearby machine is busy about seventy percent of the time. If you care about the slow replies rather than the average reply, the distant machine is ahead almost from the start. A nearby box helps the typical request. It does not help the request that arrives when everyone else is already waiting.

Some requests stay nearby, on a short wire and a slower machine. The rest travel to a faster machine.

How long a request waits. In the left panel the machines are equally busy, and they meet near seventy percent. In the middle panel they face the same flow of work, and the nearby machine falls behind sooner because that flow crowds it. The right panel is the slow replies. There the distant machine wins early.

What the lists record

The Office of Management and Budget has put out three public lists, for 2023, 2024, and 2025. A row is a form an agency filled in: a name, a short description, and a stage such as planned, pilot, or in use. Nothing on the form records how busy a machine is, how long a wire is, or whether anyone watched the system run. A longer list is a larger stack of forms.

I read the descriptions for signs that the work itself wants a nearby machine. Field kit. A dead or thin connection. A need for an answer right now. Computing on the device rather than in a hall. Few rows hit more than one of those. The share is a few percent in each year, a bit higher in 2023 than later.

Security agencies, including those that handle policing, borders, and veterans' benefits, file far more of the cases marked as touching rights or safety. In the 2024 list that gap is large: about half of their cases, against a few percent everywhere else. Their descriptions are not especially full of field work or urgent timing. The security mission and the practical case for a nearby machine are different patterns in the same forms.

Follow a name from 2024 into 2025. The dashed arrows are names that do not appear again. The step from planned or pilot into use is a thin one.

Among 2024 cases that were not yet in use, 141 that I can match are in use in 2025. Another 473 names vanish. If all of those vanished names were in use, the share would be a bit over half. If none of them were, it would be about one in eight.

What follows a year later

Of the 2024 cases I can still find in 2025, the ones whose descriptions sounded like nearby, time-critical work are rarely the ones in use. Nineteen of the matched cases that were not yet in use met that description. One of the nineteen is in use a year later. Among the other matched cases, about one in five is in use. The writeup and the later stage do not move together.

From 2023 to 2024 the lists are even harder to join. Of 709 names that were not already retired, 472 do not show up again.

The public draft is SSRN 7141719. Adding the 2025 list lengthens the record of what agencies wrote down. It does not change the hardware comparison above, because the lists never contained the speed of a machine or the length of a wire.
